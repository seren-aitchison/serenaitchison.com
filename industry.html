The part most graduates can't show

Systems that other people use.

Analysis skill is common. What is less common in a graduate is having had to keep software running when the people depending on it are not you.

  • A hostile trust boundary. ForgeLab grades code written by the person being graded, so every claim the browser makes is re-established server-side, under a permission model with no filesystem writes, no child processes and a hard runtime limit.
  • Shared architecture at scale. Gradify runs 30+ sites from a single kit. Sites consume it and never fork it, which is the only reason a fix can land everywhere at once.
  • A real database. PostgreSQL with version-controlled migrations and row-level security, not a pile of CSVs.
  • Tests that run. Including a self-test for the grader itself, because a grading system that silently breaks is worse than one that is down.

How I work

Three commitments.

01

I will tell you what the data can't support

Including when that is inconvenient for a deadline. An analysis that overstates its confidence costs far more later than one that is delivered with its caveats attached.

02

I build it so it can be re-run

Pinned environments, fixed seeds, documented provenance. The test is whether a colleague can reproduce the numbers without me in the room.

03

I can talk to the chemists

Having done synthesis and characterisation, I can explain a computational result to people who will have to act on it, and take their scepticism seriously.
