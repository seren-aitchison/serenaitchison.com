How I approach a problem

Method choices, and why.

01

Start with what would falsify it

Before choosing a method I try to write down the result that would tell me I am wrong. If no such result exists, the question is not yet specified well enough to answer.

"What would this look like if the effect were not real?"

02

Prefer the method whose assumptions I can check

A simpler model whose assumptions are inspectable usually beats a more flexible one whose failure modes are invisible. Where I do use a flexible model, I want an applicability domain around it.

"Can I tell when this model is being asked something it cannot answer?"

03

Validate on something the model has never seen

Cross-validation measures how well a procedure generalises within one dataset. Independent data is what tests whether the finding survives contact with a different cohort, platform or batch.

"Does this replicate somewhere I did not tune it?"

Independent research proposals

Thirteen proposals.

Concepts I have written independently. None has been undertaken, none is supervised, and none has results. Each names the part I expect to be genuinely difficult, because that is usually the honest measure of whether a proposal has been thought through. Several grew out of problems I ran into in my own published work — annotation drift, pharmacogene coverage, CYP2D6 structural variation.

To be clear about status. These are proposals, not findings. If a supervisor or team is interested in one, I would expect the design to change substantially on contact with people who know the field better than I do — which is rather the point of doing a PhD.
