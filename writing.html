01

What a UMAP plot does and does not preserve

Dimensionality reduction · the distance between two clusters usually means nothing

UMAP and t-SNE are everywhere in single-cell work, and both are read far more literally than they deserve. The useful mental model is that they are optimised to preserve local neighbourhoods — which points are near which other points — and that they make no promise whatsoever about global geometry.

What this rules out

  • Distance between clusters is not interpretable. Two blobs sitting far apart on a UMAP are not necessarily more different than two blobs that are close. The spacing is an artefact of the optimisation, not a measurement.
  • Cluster size is not abundance or variance. A large sprawling island may just be a region where the algorithm had room to spread points out.
  • The number of clusters is a parameter, not a finding. Change the neighbourhood size or the clustering resolution and the count changes. Reporting "we identified 14 cell types" without reporting the resolution you chose is reporting a decision as though it were a result.

What it is good for

Seeing whether structure exists at all; spotting batch effects, where samples separate by processing run rather than biology; and giving a visual handle on a clustering you have validated by other means — marker genes, known biology, or stability across parameters.

The check I would run. Re-run the embedding with a different random seed and a different neighbourhood parameter. Structure that survives both is worth discussing. Structure that does not is a picture of the algorithm, not of the tissue.
02

Why a docking score is not a binding affinity

Computational chemistry · the most common overclaim in computational drug discovery

Docking does two separable things: it generates plausible poses for a ligand in a binding site, and it scores them. It is reasonably good at the first and considerably weaker at the second, and the distinction gets lost whenever a docking score is reported in kcal/mol as though it were a measurement.

Where the scoring functions struggle

  • Entropy and desolvation. Binding involves stripping ordered water from both partners and losing conformational freedom. Scoring functions approximate these terms crudely because computing them properly is expensive.
  • Protein flexibility. Most docking treats the receptor as rigid. Real binding sites reorganise, and induced fit is exactly the case a rigid model handles worst.
  • Ranking versus prediction. A scoring function may order a congeneric series sensibly while being unreliable across chemically diverse compounds — the case where you most want it to work.

What can be said honestly

That docking generated a structural hypothesis: a pose, and a set of interactions the ligand could make if it binds in that orientation. That hypothesis is genuinely useful — it guides which analogue to make next. It is not evidence of activity, and the gap is closed by assay data, not by a better score.

Why I am insistent about this. It is the fastest way for computational work to lose the confidence of experimental colleagues. Having been on the synthesis side, I have some sense of what it costs to make the compound a docking score suggested.
03

Why genome-wide significance is 5 × 10⁻⁸

Statistics · multiple testing, and what a QQ plot is really for

Test a million independent hypotheses at p < 0.05 and you expect around fifty thousand false positives. The genome-wide threshold is a Bonferroni-style correction for roughly a million independent tests — an approximation of the number of effectively independent common variants in the human genome, given the correlation structure of linkage disequilibrium. 0.05 ÷ 10⁶ gives 5 × 10⁻⁸.

What the threshold is not

It is not a line between real and unreal. A variant at p = 6 × 10⁻⁸ is not meaningfully different from one at 4 × 10⁻⁸; the threshold controls a family-wise error rate across a scan, and that is all it does. An effect that misses it may simply be underpowered, which is why effect sizes should be reported alongside p-values rather than instead of being mentioned.

The QQ plot is the diagnostic that matters

  • Points on the diagonal: test statistics behaving as the null predicts.
  • A tail lifting away at the top: what genuine association looks like.
  • The whole line above the diagonal: inflation. Usually population structure or cryptic relatedness, occasionally a technical artefact — and almost never a genome full of real signal.

The genomic inflation factor λ summarises this as the ratio of the median observed chi-square to its null expectation. λ near 1 is what you want; λ above about 1.1 means something needs explaining before the hits are worth discussing.

See it move. The statistics studio computes λ live. Raise the inflation slider with zero true signals planted and watch the entire line lift — that is the pattern that should stop you trusting a scan.
04

SHAP explains a model, not a mechanism

Interpretability · the gap between feature importance and biology

SHAP values distribute a prediction across the input features, using an approach borrowed from cooperative game theory: each feature's contribution is its average marginal effect across orderings of the features. The mathematics is principled, and the usual interpretation of the output is not.

Three ways the biological reading goes wrong

  • Correlated features share credit arbitrarily. Genes in the same pathway are often highly correlated. The model may lean on one and effectively ignore its neighbours; SHAP will faithfully report that, and a reader will conclude the chosen gene is the important one. Swap the correlated partner in and the model performs identically.
  • Confounds get attributed as biology. If a batch effect predicts the outcome, the features carrying it will show high attribution. The model is not wrong — that feature genuinely drives its prediction — but the explanation is about the dataset, not the disease.
  • Attribution is not causal. SHAP describes a model that was fitted to observational data. Nothing in the procedure licenses an intervention claim.

How I would use it anyway

As a debugging tool first: if the top features are technical covariates or known confounds, the model has learned the wrong thing and the explanation has just saved you from publishing it. As a hypothesis generator second, where a high-attribution feature with a plausible mechanism is worth following up experimentally. Never as a finding in its own right.

The honest summary. "The model relies on gene X" and "gene X matters biologically" are different statements. Interpretability methods establish the first. Only an experiment establishes the second.
